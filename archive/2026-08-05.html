Three Claude Skills I Think Every Org Should Have

Arpit Bhayani argues that engineering organizations lose productivity not to hard problems but to tooling gaps, and recommends building three core skills. The first skill, create-app, should provision databases, wire up logging, register services with the identity provider, and deploy to production in a single command, eliminating day-one friction. The second skill, deploy, should treat forward deploys, rollbacks, environment-scoped tests, and post-deploy smoke tests as a single integrated flow, since rollback is the least exercised path despite being the most critical during incidents. The third skill, debugging, should match live symptoms to known failure patterns and link directly to logs, traces, and dashboards rather than pointing users at raw data. He emphasizes that executable tools fail loudly and get fixed, while documentation rots silently, a distinction that matters even more as AI agents begin creating services and triaging incidents. He advises starting with the worst service, the one that pages most frequently, to prove the approach before scaling across the fleet.

Some more things about Django I've been enjoying

The author has been learning to build websites using a backend-focused, "2010 style" approach with SQL databases and HTML rendered on the server, choosing this method over frontend-heavy single-page apps for sites with many pages. They are enjoying Django and highlight several features: query builder methods on QuerySet classes that make filtering readable, useful template filters like urlize, linebreaksbr, date, json_script, and querystring for generating HTML and links, and automatic database migrations. They dislike using class-based views with inheritance for organizing view code, preferring function-based views instead. On performance, they discovered they had accidentally disabled Django's cached template loader, and re-enabling it improved their site's capacity on a ~$10/month VM from about 2-3 requests per second to around 12. PULSE: An Executable Contract Language for Spatiotemporal Knowledge Graph Engineering

PULSE is an Object-Process-Methodology-inspired executable contract language that localizes four operational roles and their write effects within a single typed runtime for spatiotemporal knowledge graph engineering. Its implemented contract fixes five behaviors—evidence non-overwrite, branch isolation, grounded multi-subject timers, guarded state change, and declaration-ranked event ordering over time and space—while an external runner still decides whether evidence becomes an authoritative move, with GeoSPARQL, SOSA, and SHACL remaining generated views. The authors provide a core calculus yielding an effect-confinement lemma and six safety properties, and Lean 4 verifies kernel analogues for positions, evidence, clocks, monitors, atomicity, and branch source retention. Validation includes 88 tests, 3,534 bounded checks, 32 Lean/Python runtime-kernel cases, reproduction of a tested cold-chain trace, and 37,440 generated temporal traces matching a separate workflow while distinguishing ten single-field mutants. On the complete NOAA IBTrACS since-1980 subset, PULSE agrees with GEOS and an event sweep across 1,476,290 transition-zone pairs, including 4,800 sampled and 12,831 duration-qualified events.

LLMs reward expertise

LLMs have made everyone into generalists by filling in technical gaps like writing code or solving math problems. While some believe prompting skill alone determines results, the article argues that the most important skill in prompting is actual domain expertise. This is illustrated by Terence Tao's conversation with ChatGPT about the Jacobian Conjecture, where Tao's short, pointed messages signal expertise and push the model into a more rigorous mode, though the key to his technique is his deep mathematical understanding, not just prompting tricks. The same principle applies to other fields like software development, where familiarity with a codebase allows a person to steer the LLM more effectively by recognizing when solutions are overly complex or miss existing patterns. As models grow stronger, human expertise remains valuable because the bottleneck is often the human's ability to communicate exactly what kind of solution they want.

Xbox goes down. You can't play games you own on disc

An extended Xbox outage beginning Sunday evening, July 27, 2026, prevented users from playing both digital games and physical disc-based games. This incident highlights how owning a game on disc today functions more as a license than true ownership, since Microsoft, Sony, and Nintendo can unintentionally or intentionally block access to games. Even with a disc inserted, the game installs to the console's internal hard drive and typically requires mandatory updates to function. The author contrasts this with older physical media, noting that cartridges from devices like the Game Boy can still be played on new hardware like the Analog Pocket without requiring authorization. Because of these limitations with modern disc-based games, the author has gravitated toward PC gaming, where players have more reliable means of maintaining long-term access to their games.

Show HN: Simple algorithm and color space to generate diverse skin tones

A developer created a custom color space with simple equations to generate diverse, plausible skin tones for digital art and character creators. The methodology involved manually labeling colors in RGB, performing principal component analysis (PCA) with N=3 to simplify the dataset shape, and writing equations that map a sphere onto the transformed data in PCA space. The provided code includes functions to uniformly sample points from a sphere and convert them to RGB values using linear coefficients. The author acknowledges significant limitations, noting that real skin tones vary across the body, are affected by health conditions like argyria and bilirubin levels, and that the work is subjective and potentially affected by display and lighting differences. The article also addresses the broader social context of colorism and racism, recommending related videos and projects on inclusive representation in makeup and video games.
